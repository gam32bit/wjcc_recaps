TOPIC:

Approve Resolution R-9-26, Month of the Military Child

 

BACKGROUND:

In 1986, Secretary of Defense Caspar W. Weinberger designated April as “The Month of the Military Child” in recognition of the contributions that military children make to the community and nation. During April, all branches of service, the Commonwealth and many school divisions provide special activities, events and days to honor military-connected children and their families.

 

RATIONALE:

Military children experience frequent moves, family separations as a result of deployments as well as reintegration challenges. Attending new schools, maintaining their educational goals and making new friends are the way of life for most of our military children. As they strive to excel under these challenging circumstances, our military children and their families deserve the support and recognition of our community and our nation.

TOPIC:

Approve Resolution R-10-26, School Library Month

 

BACKGROUND:

School Library Month is the American Association of School Librarians' (AASL) celebration of school librarians and their programs. First celebrated in 1985 (as School Library Media Month; the name was changed to School Library Month in 2010), every April school librarians are encouraged to create activities to help their school and local community celebrate the essential role that strong school library programs play in a student's educational career.

 

RATIONALE:

The American Association of School Librarians (AASL) defines the school library as “a unique and essential part of a learning community.” Effective school libraries deepen students’ learning experiences.  Under the leadership of the school librarian, the library provides equitable access for teachers and students to a variety of resources and technology.  During the month of April, school systems are reminded of the special significance that school library programs and school librarians have in Virginia’s schools.

TOPIC:

Approve the Special Education Annual Plan 2026-2027 and the Title VI-B Flow-Through Funds

 

BACKGROUND:

The Special Education Annual Plan is an application for Federal Funds, under Title VI-B of Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA). The Virginia Department of Education (VDOE) provides each locality with guidelines for completing each section. WJCC utilizes this money for special education staffing.

 

RATIONALE:

The local Special Education Annual Plan contains policies and procedures that will govern special education for the 2026-2027 school year as amended by federal regulations. The Title VI-B Flow-Through Funds determine the budget for spending federal allotment as appropriated by the Virginia Department of Education.

 

SPECIAL CIRCUMSTANCE(S):

Funds allocated by this plan cover the salaries and benefits for a specified number of special education personnel. The grant amounts are based on preliminary funding allocations from the U.S. Department of Education Office of Special Education Programs for the federal fiscal year 2027. Final allocations will be released from the Virginia Department of Education as soon as the final grant award notification is received from the U.S. Department of Education.

 

COST BUDGETED:

There is no cost to this plan provided the plan is approved prior to July 1, 2026. If approval by the VDOE occurs after July 1, 2026, salaries and benefits must be paid by the local school division until approval is received.

TOPIC:

Approve the Submission of the School Improvement Plans and Grant Applications for James River and Matthew Whaley Elementary Schools 

 

BACKGROUND:

The Virginia Department of Education (VDOE) recently updated and implemented a new accreditation system. For the Federal Accountability portion, two elementary schools were designated as needing Targeted Support & Improvement (TSI):  James River and Matthew Whaley.  

 

TSI schools are identified annually and include any school with one or more underperforming subgroups scoring below the highest-performing Comprehensive Support & Improvement (CSI) school. CSI schools are identified every 3 years and include the lowest 5% of Title I schools across the state or a high school with < 67% graduation rate. TSI identifications are annual identifications with no specific exit requirements.  Schools exit this identification after one year if they are not reidentified in the following year for any student groups.   

 

Each school has developed a comprehensive improvement plan and are eligible for additional federal funding to support academic achievement.  

 

RATIONALE:

On March 3, 2026, information was presented that explained the federal identification of James River and Matthew Whaley Elementary Schools, as well as each school's improvement plan and the submission of the school improvement grant. Local school board approval is required prior to each school's submission of its improvement plan to the Virginia Department of Education (VDOE).
